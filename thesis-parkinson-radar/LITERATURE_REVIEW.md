# Literature review & assessment of our approach

*Synthesis from a verified-source deep-research workflow (15 papers). Companion: `REFERENCES.md` (annotated), `references.bib` (citations).*

## Field overview

Radar-based gait analysis for neurological screening sits at the intersection of contactless sensing (FMCW/CW micro-Doppler radar) and machine learning. Micro-Doppler spectrograms encode torso and limb velocity signatures over the gait cycle; because radar is contactless, privacy-preserving, and independent of lighting/clothing, it is attractive for continuous in-home monitoring versus wearables or cameras. The literature closest to this thesis is small and clusters around 24 GHz Doppler radar of walking, splitting into two lines: (1) signal-processing/validation studies establishing which biomechanical gait parameters radar can reliably measure (Seifert et al. 2020; Lopez-Delgado et al. 2026, the paper behind our dataset's hardware), and (2) classification studies that discriminate coarse gait groups — almost always young-vs-elderly aging rather than a specific pathology (Hayashi/Saho et al. 2021; Hoshiga/Saho et al. 2021), using either LSTMs on extracted velocity envelopes or CNNs on full spectrograms. Critically, no published work performs deep-learning Parkinson's-vs-control classification from radar micro-Doppler with subject-independent validation: the reference hardware paper validates parameter extraction in a PD cohort but contains no classifier, and the classification papers target aging. Reported accuracies in the aging line are high (90-98%) but are repeatedly undermined by validation shortcuts (random hold-out with possible subject leakage) and confounds (groups collected at different sites; speed/geometry dependence) — one paper explicitly demonstrates its own CNN keyed on a nuisance rather than gait. Methodological rigor (subject-independent CV, confound control, interpretability) is therefore the central unsolved problem in this niche, and precisely where this thesis is positioned.

## Themes

### Radar micro-Doppler as a contactless gait-sensing modality
These works establish the physical substrate our thesis relies on. A 24 GHz Doppler radar captures a torso band (<=200 Hz, <=1 m/s) and a faster foot band (200-500 Hz), giving a contactless, privacy-preserving, lighting/clothing-robust view of gait. Seifert et al. rigorously validate radar against gold-standard motion capture + ground reaction forces and show radar reliably measures spatiotemporal rhythm/pace parameters (stride/step time, cadence, stride/step length) but that kinematic joint velocities and gait-event timing are strongly geometry-, view-, and speed-dependent. Lopez-Delgado et al. (the paper that produced our multi-node FMCW radar network and the SNR-combined foot/torso spectrograms we use) validate parameter extraction in both healthy and PD populations. Together they define what signal is physically present in our spectrograms and which parts are confounded by geometry and speed.

Papers: Doppler Radar for the Extraction of Biomechanical Parameters in Gait Analysis, Radar Network for Gait Monitoring: Technology and Validation

### Deep learning versus handcrafted features on micro-Doppler gait
Two representational strategies compete. The handcrafted route extracts low-dimensional descriptors: Hayashi/Saho extract three 1-D velocity envelopes (upper = swing/max velocity, lower = stance/min velocity, mean = torso) and feed them to an LSTM; Seifert extracts spatiotemporal and kinematic parameters directly. The data-driven route feeds full 2-D spectrograms to CNNs (Hayashi's AlexNet comparison; Hoshiga/Saho's deep model on radar signals simulated from a motion-capture gait database). Seifert explicitly frames end-to-end deep learning that maps micro-Doppler to gait structure as the open future direction, the gap our CNN fills. Evidence is mixed and instructive: Hayashi's envelope-LSTM (94.9%) was judged more trustworthy than their higher-scoring spectrogram-CNN because the CNN exploited an artifact, directly motivating our two-track design (interpretable classical baseline + CNN) and the value of adding an envelope-LSTM baseline.

Papers: Utilization of Micro-Doppler Radar to Classify Gait Patterns of Young and Elderly Adults: An Approach Using a Long Short-Term Memory Network, Simulation study of deep-learning-based gait classification of young/elderly adults using Doppler radar, Doppler Radar for the Extraction of Biomechanical Parameters in Gait Analysis

### Binary gait-group classification and the aging analog to Parkinson's
The closest published classification task is binary young-vs-elderly aging, not disease. Hayashi/Saho (real 24 GHz radar, 300 subjects) and Hoshiga/Saho (radar synthesized from the AIST optical motion-capture gait database) both perform binary aging classification and report high accuracy. This relevance cuts both ways: it is the nearest modality-and-task match to our PD-vs-control problem, but it is also the exact confound our task faces, since PD patients are typically older than controls, so a PD classifier trained on a non-age-matched cohort can silently become an age classifier and look identical on every metric. The absence of any radar-micro-Doppler PD classifier with subject-independent validation is the specific gap this thesis fills.

Papers: Utilization of Micro-Doppler Radar to Classify Gait Patterns of Young and Elderly Adults: An Approach Using a Long Short-Term Memory Network, Simulation study of deep-learning-based gait classification of young/elderly adults using Doppler radar

### Confounds, shortcut learning, and validation rigor
The field's recurring failure mode is a classifier that scores well by exploiting a group-correlated nuisance rather than gait. Hayashi/Saho is the canonical cautionary tale: young and elderly data were recorded at different sites, and their AlexNet spectrogram-CNN reached 97.8% by keying on background-noise/site differences, which the authors themselves flag as unreliable. Validation in this line is usually random hold-out (Hayashi's 7:3 over 30 splits), which risks subject leakage. Seifert adds that walking speed and radar view strongly shift the signatures, further confounds. This theme is the backbone of our approach and where our protocol already exceeds the published work: we measured a strong subject fingerprint (within/between distance ratio 0.374, all 58/58 subjects below the diagonal) proving LOSO is mandatory; we report a duration-only nuisance floor next to every model; we neutralize the trial-duration confound three independent ways (per-test split, residualization, caliper matching) and by construction via fixed 3 s windows; and we plan Grad-CAM to confirm attention on gait bands rather than artifacts.

Papers: Utilization of Micro-Doppler Radar to Classify Gait Patterns of Young and Elderly Adults: An Approach Using a Long Short-Term Memory Network, Doppler Radar for the Extraction of Biomechanical Parameters in Gait Analysis

### Clinical and biomechanical basis for PD detection from gait radar
PD gait is characterized by bradykinesia (slower, smaller foot swings, hence lower peak foot Doppler), reduced stride regularity/rhythm, and left-right asymmetry. Lopez-Delgado et al. provide the mechanistic hook this thesis leans on: the torso-velocity to heel-strike coupling that holds in healthy gait breaks down in PD subjects, a shape-and-timing signature that lives in the 2-D (Doppler x time) spectrogram and is largely discarded by scalar summary features, which is a concrete argument for a 2-D CNN over handcrafted features. Seifert complements this by establishing that radar reliably captures exactly the rhythm/pace parameters (cadence, stride/step time) most affected in PD, while cautioning that knee-derived kinematics are geometry-dependent and should be treated carefully.

Papers: Radar Network for Gait Monitoring: Technology and Validation, Doppler Radar for the Extraction of Biomechanical Parameters in Gait Analysis

## Is our approach right? (grounded assessment)

The approach is fundamentally sound and, on the evaluation axis, more rigorous than the closest published work, but it has one implementation bug and one uncontrolled confound that gate any credible positive result. WHAT IS RIGHT: (1) The cross-validation unit is correct and empirically justified, not assumed. baseline.py uses LeaveOneGroupOut on subject_id with subject-level scoring (trial probabilities averaged to one per held-out subject), and the decision was backed by measuring the subject fingerprint (within/between distance ratio 0.374, 58/58 subjects below the diagonal). This is exactly the discipline Hayashi/Saho lacked (7:3 random hold-out, subject-leakage risk, an AlexNet that hit 97.8% on a site nuisance). (2) A duration-only nuisance floor is reported beside every model (AUC 0.610), the honest defense the literature keeps failing to mount. (3) The label-permutation test shuffles at the subject level and re-runs the full LOSO, and both the favorable (residualized, p=0.022) and unfavorable (raw, p=0.092) results are reported. (4) Bootstrap CIs resample subjects, not trials, and are reported honestly wide. (5) Preprocessing (imputation, scaling) lives inside the sklearn Pipeline, so no leakage in the classical track. (6) Confound control is triangulated (per-test split, residualization, caliper matching with a caliper-robustness sweep 0.613-0.624), far stronger than one lucky configuration. (7) The fixed-3 s-window design removes the duration confound structurally rather than by statistical patching (cleaner than residualizing), and the Grad-CAM plan directly targets Hayashi's shortcut-learning failure mode. WHAT IS WRONG / RISKS: (A) CRITICAL, and confirmed in src/train.py: train_one_fold early-stops and restores best_state by maximizing the AUC computed on val_ds, and loso_cv sets val_ds to the single held-out subject. The returned subject prediction therefore comes from the checkpoint that maximizes that same test subject's window AUC, i.e. model selection on the test set. Because the deep track has not run yet, no published number is wrong, but as written any CNN/ResNet AUC would be optimistically biased and invalid. This must be fixed before running (hold out a separate validation subject/subset from the training folds for early stopping, or use a fixed epoch budget). (B) HIGH: the age confound is entirely uncontrolled: no age/sex/UPDRS/medication metadata yet, and an unresolved young/older split inside the controls. Since PD patients are typically older and micro-Doppler gait is strongly age-dependent (this is literally Hayashi's task), a PD-vs-control model could be an age classifier and look identical on every metric; duration matching does not touch age. Until an age-matched analysis is possible, no positive result (classical or deep) can be claimed as PD-specific. (C) MEDIUM: the headline p=0.022 was selected after a p=0.092 configuration and across many feature-set x model x test-split x transform combinations with no multiplicity correction; a single p just under 0.05 across ~15+ AUC computations is fragile and should be reported with that caveat (or a BH/Bonferroni correction). Minor notes: ResNet-18 ImageNet transfer to 2-channel Doppler spectrograms is reasonable but faces a domain gap in a small-n regime; the adaptive per-trial contrast-enhancement (formula unknown, dB-fit R2 0.46-0.95 across trials) makes absolute-brightness/energy comparisons unsafe and should be treated as a preprocessing unknown to resolve with the data owner.

### What to adopt from the literature

- Keep the subject-independent LOSO spine with subject-level probability aggregation, subject-resampled bootstrap CIs, and subject-level label-permutation testing; it already exceeds the closest published protocols.
- Keep reporting a nuisance-only (duration-only) floor next to every model; extend the same discipline to the CNN (report duration-matched and test2-only CNN AUCs, not just pooled).
- Add an interpretable envelope-based baseline in the style of Hayashi/Saho: extract upper (swing), lower (stance), and mean (torso) Doppler envelopes and feed a sequence model (LSTM); test whether the stance/lower-envelope carries most of the PD signal, as it did for aging.
- Use Grad-CAM to verify the CNN attends to physiologically meaningful gait bands (foot 200-500 Hz, torso <=200 Hz) rather than artifacts, the direct antidote to Hayashi's shortcut-learning failure.
- Record and, where possible, control radar geometry and walking speed as covariates (Seifert shows kinematic velocities and event timing are view- and speed-dependent); be cautious with knee/high-velocity-derived features.
- Exploit the physically grounded band priors and the torso-heel-strike coupling breakdown from Lopez-Delgado/Seifert for feature design, ablations, and interpretation sanity checks.
- Retain the fixed-length-window design as the primary structural fix for the duration confound, and keep the foot-only/torso-only/both and abs-Doppler-folding ablations that the data findings support.

### What to avoid

- Do not use random or record-wise (trial-level) cross-validation; our measured subject fingerprint (within/between ratio 0.374, 58/58) shows it would massively inflate AUC, the leakage risk in Hayashi's 7:3 hold-out.
- Do not select the model checkpoint / early-stop on the held-out test subject (the current src/train.py behavior); this is model selection on the test set and would invalidate any reported CNN/ResNet AUC. Use a nested validation subject or a fixed epoch budget.
- Do not report a spectrogram-CNN number without confirming it is not riding a confound; Hayashi's AlexNet hit 97.8% purely on a site/background nuisance.
- Do not claim a PD-specific result without age control; without age-matching the classifier could be an age detector (Hayashi's exact task) and would look identical on every metric.
- Do not over-read the learning-curve endpoint (~0.72 at n=50, high variance) or the single p=0.022 without multiplicity correction across the many feature/model/split/transform combinations tried.
- Do not compare absolute brightness or energy across contrast-enhanced spectrograms (adaptive per-trial CE, unknown formula) or trust duration-leaking energy-sum features; these are artifacts, not gait.
- Do not chase inflated accuracy; a well-validated 0.7-0.8 that survives duration-matched and test2-only confound checks is a stronger thesis result than an unvalidated 0.9+ that does not.

### Gaps our thesis can fill

- No published radar micro-Doppler classifier for Parkinson's-vs-control with subject-independent validation exists; the closest works classify aging (young/elderly) or only measure gait parameters. This thesis fills the PD-classification gap directly on validated hardware.
- The reference hardware paper (Lopez-Delgado et al. 2026) validates gait-parameter extraction in a PD cohort but contains no classifier; adding an end-to-end learned PD classifier on top of the validated radar network is a genuine contribution.
- Prior deep-learning micro-Doppler gait work relies on random/record-wise hold-out (subject-leakage risk); subject-independent LOSO reported with bootstrap CIs and subject-level label-permutation tests is essentially absent, a validation-rigor gap this thesis addresses.
- No prior radar-gait study systematically neutralizes the dominant confounds. Hayashi documents a site confound but does not remove it; none report a nuisance-only floor. Our triangulated duration control (matched/residualized/test-split + duration-only baseline + fixed-window design) is itself a methodological contribution.
- Age-matched PD radar-gait analysis with UPDRS/medication metadata is missing across the field; cohorts are small, able-bodied, or aging-based. A properly age-controlled PD analysis would be novel (contingent on obtaining the metadata).
- No prior radar-gait PD work links CNN attention back to clinically established gait bands (foot 200-500 Hz, torso <=200 Hz) via Grad-CAM to demonstrate the model attends to gait rather than artifacts.
- The clinically motivated torso-velocity to heel-strike coupling breakdown in PD (noted by Lopez-Delgado) has not been exploited as a learned classification signal; testing whether a 2-D CNN recovers it is an open, mechanistically grounded question.

### Final verdict

This thesis is methodologically well-founded and, on evaluation discipline, already more rigorous than the closest published radar micro-Doppler gait work: it correctly and empirically justifies subject-independent LOSO, reports a nuisance-only floor beside every model, triangulates the duration confound three ways plus a structural fixed-window fix, and uses subject-level permutation tests and bootstrap CIs, directly answering the shortcut-learning and subject-leakage failures that inflate the young/elderly aging literature (Hayashi's 97.8% site artifact, 7:3 hold-outs), while filling a real gap since no published deep-learning PD-vs-control radar classifier with subject-independent validation exists. Two issues, however, gate any credible positive claim and must be resolved first: the CNN training loop currently early-stops on the held-out test subject (confirmed in src/train.py), which would make any deep AUC optimistically biased and invalid until fixed with a proper nested validation split; and the age confound is entirely uncontrolled, so without age-matched metadata a PD-vs-control model cannot be distinguished from an age classifier, the single largest threat to validity. With those fixed and multiplicity acknowledged around the p=0.022 result, the honest expectation is a modest but defensible subject-level AUC in the ~0.62-0.7 range that survives the confound checks, which is a stronger and more publishable contribution than the inflated numbers common in this niche.

## Reviewer panel (adversarial lenses)

### Lens: Radar signal domain — micro-Doppler representation, foot/torso fusion, contrast enhancement, windowing, augmentation
**Verdict:** sound-with-caveats — The core representational spine (2-channel foot+torso micro-Doppler, fixed 3 s windows to kill trial-duration leakage, subject-level LOSO with window-probability averaging, planned ce-vs-raw / foot-vs-torso / window-length ablations, and Grad-CAM verification) is well-motivated and directly aligned with the cited radar-gait literature, and the team is unusually confound-aware. But three radar-domain choices are live hazards rather than settled: (1) the input is the adaptive, per-trial, unknown-formula contrast-enhanced (ce) spectrogram, which is the exact confound-leakage channel that sank Hayashi et al.'s spectrogram CNN; (2) the decision that Doppler is folded/symmetric rests on a statistically invalid test, risking destruction of real directional/asymmetry signal; and (3) established PD markers (left/right asymmetry) are discarded by using only the pre-SNR-combined nodes, and augmentation is minimal for an admittedly data-limited problem. All are fixable without changing the thesis thesis.

Strengths:
- Fixed 3 s windowing (src/preprocessing.py, window_s=3.0) genuinely removes the *total-trial-duration* confound by construction — a window is the same length whether the trial was 9 s or 19 s. This is the single strongest radar-domain methodological move and correctly targets the confound the classical baseline exposed (RESULTS.md 2, FINDINGS.md 7). 3 s also spans ~2-3 gait cycles, consistent with Hayashi (2 steady-state cycles) and Seifert (~2 cycles).
- The 2-channel [ce_foot, ce_torso] stack (src/channels.py) is well-justified: the reference paper (Lopez-Delgado) reports that the torso-velocity<->heel-strike coupling *breaks down in PD*, which is a cross-node relationship a 2-channel CNN can see but per-channel scalar features cannot (RESULTS.md 4).
- Per-window log1p+z-score normalisation (normalise_window) is leak-safe (each window uses only its own statistics, no cross-subject/train-test leakage) and removes per-window global brightness, partially neutralising the adaptive-CE brightness offset.
- Doppler bands are physically grounded and used consistently (radar_params.py: torso 0-200 Hz, foot 200-500 Hz; velocity conversion 0.0065 m/s per Hz), and QC (src/qc.py) validates Doppler range, 5 Hz bin spacing, NaN/Inf and flat-line dynamic range — solid signal-integrity hygiene.
- The team already treats adaptive CE as 'a trap we have to control for' (CONCEPTS.md 4-6) and has planned the two verifications that most directly address the Hayashi failure mode: the ce-vs-raw |stft| ablation and Grad-CAM to confirm the CNN attends to the foot/torso Doppler bands rather than artifacts (RESULTS.md 5). The FFT is 4x zero-padded (5 Hz bins over a true 20 Hz resolution, radar_params.py), so resizing 320->224 Doppler bins discards no real resolution.

Risks / mistakes:
- ADAPTIVE CONTRAST ENHANCEMENT IS A CONFOUND-LEAKAGE CHANNEL. ce_foot/ce_torso are rescaled per-trial to each recording's own dynamic range (FINDINGS.md 1: not element-wise, dB-like + per-trial/per-channel rescale, R^2 0.46-0.95; CONCEPTS.md 5: 'ce rescales each recording to its own brightness range... the control recording had more background noise, so the enhancement lit the whole image up'). This means recording-level noise-floor/SNR/distance/body-size differences are baked into the image texture. If those differ systematically between PD and control sessions, the CNN can key on them instead of gait — precisely the failure Hayashi et al. document, where an AlexNet hit 97.8% by latching onto site/background-noise differences rather than gait. The formula is unknown and effectively non-invertible, so ce amplitudes are not physically meaningful or comparable across recordings.
- THE DOPPLER-FOLDING CONCLUSION RESTS ON AN INVALID TEST. FINDINGS.md 6 concludes ce_foot is Doppler-symmetric ('direction not preserved', median E(+)/E(-)=1.005) and recommends folding to 160 abs-Doppler bins. But that statistic is whole-trial energy balance, and a TUG walk goes out-and-back with a turn — so equal +/- energy is expected *even if instantaneous direction is fully preserved*. The test cannot distinguish physical magnitude-folding from a direction-preserving signal balanced over a there-and-back walk. This directly contradicts CONCEPTS.md 2, which claims the toward/away turn is visible as an up/down Doppler flip. Folding to abs-Doppler on this basis could destroy real directional and approach-vs-return asymmetry signal. Torso was never even tested.
- LEFT/RIGHT GAIT ASYMMETRY IS DISCARDED. The pipeline consumes only the pre-SNR-combined ce_foot (2 foot radars already fused) and ce_torso. Gait asymmetry is one of the most established PD markers (flagged in CONCEPTS.md 3 and the aging/PD literature), yet the combined + possibly folded representation cannot express it. The per-radar arrays (all_doppler_time, all_ce_time, idx_foot/torso_max_snr) exist in the .mat (data_loader.py KEYS_PER_RADAR) but are unused.
- WINDOWING REMOVES DURATION LEAKAGE BUT NOT NON-GAIT-CONTENT LEAKAGE. TUG contains sit-to-stand, standing and turning phases; a 3 s window can fall entirely on a non-gait phase yet inherit the trial's PD label (label noise). More subtly, the model can key on the *proportion of low-Doppler standing/turning content* in a window, which differs by group via the same mechanism as the duration confound — and that confound lives in exactly test1's chair-stand phase (FINDINGS.md 7: +10.1% in test1 vs +3.4% in test2). So 'windows remove the duration confound by construction' (RESULTS.md 4) is only true for *total* duration, not for within-window activity composition.
- AUGMENTATION IS MINIMAL AND NOT RADAR-TAILORED FOR A DATA-LIMITED PROBLEM. dataset.py _augment only does time-flip (p=0.5) and additive Gaussian noise sigma=0.02. On z-scored (unit-std) data, 2% noise is negligible. Time-flip reverses the accelerate-then-decelerate swing scallop into a physically implausible shape, and if Doppler direction is in fact preserved (see risk 2) it produces impossible signatures (forward motion, reversed foot kinematics). Meanwhile the learning curve is still rising at n=58 (RESULTS.md 3c) — the regime where physically-grounded augmentation would help most — yet velocity/Doppler-scaling, cadence time-warp, SNR/clutter noise and spec-style time/Doppler masking are all absent.
- NORMALISATION IS REPRESENTATION-AGNOSTIC AND DOUBLY APPLIED. normalise_window applies the same log1p+z-score whether the input is already-log-compressed ce (range ~[0,1], so log1p is nearly a no-op) or linear-magnitude |stft| (range up to ~138, where log1p is appropriate). Applying log to already-log data is conceptually confused; the log stage should be conditioned on the representation.
- SECONDARY: (a) radar_params.py still uses F_CARRIER=23.0 GHz / BW=1.4 GHz, but the measured capture is 23.5 GHz / 1.38 GHz (FINDINGS.md 3), a ~2% error in every Doppler->velocity conversion, still not fixed. (b) resize_2d uses torch bilinear without antialias=True and squashes a variable-width 3 s slice into a square 224x224 — likely benign because the 98.75% STFT overlap (1-sample hop, radar_params.py) makes native time bins hugely oversampled (true resolution 50 ms), but worth verifying. (c) channels.py 'diff' mode computes foot minus torso *after* each is independently z-scored, so it is not a physical asymmetry map. (d) ResNet-18 ImageNet transfer feeds 2-channel z-scored radar into filters/BN tuned to natural-image RGB statistics — a real domain gap that caps transfer benefit and makes the planned Grad-CAM check essential, not optional.

Recommendations:
- Treat raw |stft_foot|/|stft_torso| as the primary input and ce as the ablation, not the reverse. Replace adaptive per-trial CE with a FIXED, global normalisation (e.g. log or dB compression with a fixed noise-floor and fixed clip range derived once on the training folds only), so the per-recording noise floor cannot encode group identity. Keep the ce-vs-raw ablation but report it as a confound test, not a performance nicety.
- Run an explicit leakage probe: train the same LOSO classifier on noise-floor / SNR / background-region statistics ALONE (e.g. energy in an empty Doppler band, per-trial dynamic range, ce rescale parameters). If it reaches meaningful AUC, the ce representation is leaking recording metadata a la Hayashi, and any CNN result on ce is suspect until Grad-CAM confirms gait-band attention.
- Redo the Doppler-direction test correctly before deciding to fold: compute the sign of the per-time-frame (or per-window) Doppler centroid, not whole-trial +/- energy, on BOTH ce_foot and ce_torso. Resolve the CONCEPTS.md-vs-FINDINGS.md contradiction empirically. Only fold to 160 abs-Doppler bins if per-frame direction is genuinely absent; otherwise keep signed Doppler so approach/return and directional asymmetry survive.
- Recover left/right asymmetry: load the per-radar arrays (all_ce_time / all_doppler_time, idx_foot_max_snr) and build an asymmetry-aware channel or feature (e.g. per-leg envelope contrast), since asymmetry is a strong PD marker the SNR-combined input currently discards. Ask Ignacio for the per-node layout to do this correctly.
- Add gait-activity gating and phase-aware analysis to the windowing: threshold/weight windows by foot-band (200-500 Hz) energy so pure standing/turning windows do not inject label noise, and report results split by within-trial window position and test1-vs-test2 so reviewers can see the chair-stand phase is not carrying a disguised duration/activity confound. Prefer test2 for the cleanest gait claim (RESULTS.md already leans this way).
- Strengthen augmentation with radar-physical transforms in this data-limited regime: Doppler/velocity scaling (simulate walking-speed variation), mild cadence time-warp, SNR/clutter noise matched to the radar noise model, and SpecAugment-style time/Doppler masking. Raise the additive-noise sigma to a level that actually perturbs unit-std inputs, and drop or justify time-flip given the direction question. Make the log stage representation-aware (log for linear |stft|, identity for already-log ce). Also update radar_params.py to 23.5 GHz / 1.38 GHz, set antialias=True in resize_2d (or use area/max resampling to preserve foot-spike peaks), and relabel the channels.py 'diff' mode since it is not a physical asymmetry after independent z-scoring.

### Lens: Statistical rigor: sample size, LOSO vs record-wise CV, confounds, overfitting, permutation testing, honest reporting.
**Verdict:** sound-with-caveats

Strengths:
- CV UNIT IS CORRECT AND EMPIRICALLY JUSTIFIED. baseline.py uses LeaveOneGroupOut on subject_id with subject-level scoring (trial probs averaged to one score per held-out subject, loso_subject_scores, lines 95-114). This is exactly the discipline the closest-modality paper lacked: Hayashi/Saho (24 GHz micro-Doppler, young-vs-elderly) used a 7:3 random hold-out over 30 splits, risking subject leakage, and their AlexNet spectrogram-CNN hit 97.8% by keying on a group-correlated nuisance. Critically, the project did NOT assume LOSO is needed - it measured it: median within/between-subject distance 0.374 with 58/58 subjects below the diagonal (FINDINGS.md sec 8). That fingerprint proves record-wise/trial-level CV would massively inflate, so LOSO is not optional here. This is the single most important thing to get right, and it is right.
- A DURATION-ONLY CONFOUND FLOOR IS REPORTED NEXT TO EVERY MODEL. baseline.feature_sets includes duration_only=[duration_s] (line 86), and RESULTS.md sec 3 shows the confound-controlled gait features (shape_clean 0.621) beat trial length alone (0.610) by only ~0.01. Explicitly reporting the nuisance-only floor next to the real model is exemplary honest reporting and is precisely the defense against the failure mode the literature warns about (a classifier that looks good but rides a group-correlated artifact).
- PERMUTATION TEST IS METHODOLOGICALLY CORRECT. permutation_pvalue (baseline.py 135-151) shuffles labels at the SUBJECT level and maps them back to trials, preserving within-subject block structure (a trial-level shuffle would break the group structure and give an anti-conservative null); it re-runs the FULL LOSO per shuffle, uses the +1/(n+1) estimator over 1000 shuffles, and reports one-sided p. They also honestly reported the unfavorable raw result (p=0.092) alongside the favorable one, rather than burying it.
- RESAMPLING UNIT FOR CIs IS CORRECT. subject_metrics bootstraps by resampling SUBJECTS (not trials) for the AUC CI (baseline.py 121-123, BOOTSTRAP_RESAMPLES=2000), which is the right unit given clustered data, and the resulting CIs are reported honestly wide (e.g. [0.47, 0.77]) rather than hidden.
- NO PREPROCESSING LEAKAGE IN THE CLASSICAL TRACK. Imputation and StandardScaler live inside the sklearn Pipeline (baseline._models), so Pipeline.fit is called on X[tr] only within each LOSO fold - scaler/imputer statistics never see the held-out subject.
- CONFOUND CONTROL IS TRIANGULATED, NOT A SINGLE FRAGILE TEST. confound_analysis.py runs three orthogonal duration controls (per-test split, residualization, greedy caliper matching to post-match MWU p=0.95) that agree, and diagnostics.py adds a caliper-robustness sweep (AUC 0.613-0.624 across 0.25-2.0 s) showing the matched result is not a caliper artifact. Convergent evidence is far stronger than one lucky configuration.
- THE CNN'S CONFOUND FIX IS STRUCTURAL, NOT A STATISTICAL PATCH. Fixed 3 s windows make trial length constant by construction, so duration cannot leak into the per-window model - a cleaner solution than residualizing it away. The plan to run Grad-CAM to confirm attention on gait bands (foot 200-500 Hz, torso <=200 Hz) directly addresses the Hayashi cautionary tale of a CNN latching onto artifacts.
- HONEST ABOUT ITS OWN WEAK EVIDENCE. RESULTS.md flags that the learning-curve endpoint (0.72 at n=50) is high-variance and should not be over-read, and that the RF permutation-importance ~=0 reflects an overfit RF - it explicitly tells the reader to lean on the matched/residualized evidence instead. This calibrated self-skepticism is rare and correct.

Risks / mistakes:
- CRITICAL - TEST-SET LEAKAGE IN THE (NOT-YET-RUN) CNN VIA EARLY STOPPING. In train.py, loso_cv sets val_ds to the held-out subject (line 135), then train_one_fold early-stops on and restores best_state by MAXIMIZING that held-out subject's window AUC (lines 86-95: 'if auc > best_auc: best_state=...'), and finally reports that same subject's prediction. The returned best_val_window_auc is literally the max-over-epochs of the test subject's AUC. This is model selection on the test set: the reported subject-level CNN AUC will be optimistically biased, potentially by a large margin. It is the deep-learning analogue of the record-wise-CV inflation the project correctly avoided in the classical track. Because the deep track has not been run yet (RESULTS.md sec 1), no published number is wrong yet - but as written, any CNN/ResNet result it produces would be invalid.
- HIGH - AGE CONFOUND IS ENTIRELY UNCONTROLLED AND IS THE LARGEST THREAT TO VALIDITY. No age/sex/UPDRS metadata exists yet, and the dataset even has an unresolved young(G1)/older(G2) split inside the controls (project_summary line 42). PD subjects are typically older than controls, and micro-Doppler gait is strongly age-dependent - this is literally the Hayashi young-vs-elderly task. Their spectrogram-CNN reached 97.8% by exploiting a group-correlated nuisance rather than the target. A PD-vs-control CNN on this cohort could be an age classifier and would look identical in every metric reported. Duration matching does NOT touch age. Until an age-matched analysis is possible, no positive result (classical or deep) can be claimed as PD-specific.
- MEDIUM - MULTIPLICITY / GARDEN OF FORKING PATHS AROUND THE p=0.022 'CAPSTONE'. The headline significant result (residualized shape_clean, AUC 0.676, p=0.022) was selected after the pre-residualization configuration gave p=0.092, and after trying multiple feature sets x 3 models x 3 test splits x 3 confound transforms (baseline.py + confound_analysis.py). Reporting one configuration's p=0.022 with no multiplicity correction overstates significance; across ~15+ AUC computations a single p just under 0.05 is fragile and would not survive Bonferroni/BH. As written, 0.022 reads as confirmatory when it is exploratory.
- MEDIUM - RESIDUALIZATION AND FEATURE SELECTION ARE FIT OUTSIDE THE CV LOOP. residualize_on_duration (confound_analysis.py 81-93) fits per-feature duration regressions on ALL trials, including the held-out subject, before LOSO is run on the residuals; likewise the shape_clean set is chosen using |spearman(feature,duration)|<0.3 computed on the full dataset (baseline.feature_sets 79-80). Neither uses the class label, so label-discrimination inflation is small, but strictly both transforms should be fit inside each fold. The residualized AUC 0.676 is therefore a mildly optimistic out-of-sample estimate.
- MEDIUM (INHERENT) - n=58 (33 control / 25 PD) IS UNDERPOWERED AND LOSO AUC IS HIGH-VARIANCE. Subject-level CIs already span chance (e.g. [0.47, 0.81]); a single flipped subject changes the LOSO ROC noticeably at this n. A future CNN claim of 0.70-0.80 will carry a CI of roughly +/-0.12-0.15 and cannot be cleanly separated from the ~0.62 confound floor. Any bare point estimate would be misleading at this sample size.
- MEDIUM - CNN HYPERPARAMETER-SELECTION PROTOCOL IS UNDEFINED, RISKING META-LEVEL TUNING ON TEST. Architecture, lr, epochs, batch size, augmentation, channel_mode (dataset.py supports both/foot/torso/both_3ch/diff) and window length are all free knobs. If any are chosen by inspecting the LOSO AUC over the same 58 subjects, that is selection-on-test one level up, compounding the early-stopping leak above.
- LOW - SINGLE-SEED DEEP RUNS. RANDOM_STATE is fixed at 0 everywhere and train.py uses one seed for init/augmentation, so run-to-run variance of the CNN would go unreported - unacceptable at n=58 where seed noise can rival the effect size.

Recommendations:
- FIX THE CNN EARLY-STOPPING LEAK BEFORE RUNNING THE DEEP TRACK. In train.py, do not use the held-out subject as the early-stopping signal. Carve an inner validation set drawn ONLY from the training subjects (nested LOSO / grouped inner split), select best_state and epoch on that inner val, and touch the held-out subject exactly once for final scoring. Alternatively fix an epoch budget a priori. Rename best_val_window_auc so it can never be confused with a test metric.
- TREAT AGE AS A PRIMARY CONFOUND, NOT an afterthought. Obtain the age/sex/UPDRS/medication table (already on the request list - escalate it, it gates the whole claim), then (a) report an age-only baseline (predict PD from age) as a floor exactly as duration_only is used today, and (b) report an age-matched-subset AUC. State prominently in the thesis that current results cannot rule out age as the driver.
- PRE-SPECIFY ONE PRIMARY ANALYSIS AND LABEL THE REST EXPLORATORY. Fix, before running, a single primary CNN configuration (one channel_mode, one architecture, LOSO, subject-mean probability, AUC + subject-bootstrap CI + subject-level permutation p). Report the family of other configs together and apply a BH/Bonferroni correction so p=0.022 is presented as exploratory-supportive, not confirmatory.
- MOVE RESIDUALIZATION AND shape_clean SELECTION INSIDE THE CV FOLD. Fit the per-feature duration regression and the |corr(duration)|<0.3 selection on training subjects only within each LOSO fold, then apply to the held-out subject. Re-report the residualized AUC (expect a small drop from 0.676) and re-run the permutation on the leakage-free pipeline.
- APPLY THE SAME CONFOUND BATTERY TO THE CNN THAT WAS APPLIED TO THE BASELINE. Run test2-only, duration-matched, and age-matched evaluations plus the subject-level permutation and bootstrap on the deep models, and report mean +/- sd over >=5 seeds. A CNN number without these checks is not comparable to the classical floor.
- FRAME THE TARGET HONESTLY GIVEN n=58. The win condition is a well-validated 0.70-0.80 that survives duration-matched + test2-only + age-matched + permutation with a reported CI - not a bare AUC. Consider reporting a lower-variance estimator (repeated subject-grouped k-fold or leave-pair-out) alongside LOSO to stabilize the point estimate.
- GATE ANY HIGH CNN AUC ON A GRAD-CAM/OCCLUSION SANITY CHECK. Before trusting a strong result, confirm the CNN attends to the physiologically expected Doppler bands (foot 200-500 Hz, torso <=200 Hz) rather than edges/artifacts - this is the direct, concrete lesson from the Hayashi CNN that scored 97.8% on the wrong signal.


### Lens: clinical validity
**Verdict:** sound-with-caveats — the evaluation protocol is unusually honest for this field, but without age, sex, medication-state and severity metadata the ~0.62 AUC cannot yet be claimed as a PD signal rather than an age/site artifact.

Strengths:
- Timed-Up-and-Go is a validated PD mobility/fall-risk assessment; its turn and sit-to-stand phases are exactly where PD festination, freezing and postural instability manifest.
- Subject-level LOSO is the correct, honest choice — the literature shows individual identity is strongly encoded in micro-Doppler (Papanastasiou >93.5% ID on 22 subjects; Ni & Huang 96.7% on 20), so any subject-sharing split lets the model memorize people.
- Confound discipline (3 s windows, duration-matched + test2-only controls, permutation test, bootstrap CI, reporting a weak-but-real 0.62–0.68) directly answers the field's central failure mode — e.g. Hayashi's AlexNet hitting 97.8% off a site artifact.
- Channel choice is defensible: Hoshiga shows leg motion carries the discriminative signal (leg-only 91%); torso+foot can express the torso/heel-strike coupling the reference work says breaks down in PD.

Risks / mistakes:
- **Age is the dominant uncontrolled confound.** Hayashi classifies young-vs-elderly from the same 24 GHz micro-Doppler at 94.9%; PD subjects are typically older than controls, so with no age metadata a 0.62 AUC could substantially be an age classifier — currently untestable and the most likely benign explanation.
- **Medication ON/OFF state is unrecorded.** Levodopa dramatically changes PD gait; mixed/unlogged state makes the "PD" label biologically ambiguous.
- **Radar measures the most diagnostic PD feature worst.** Gurbuz shows radar captures cadence well but gait variability (the parkinsonian hallmark) poorly; per-window z-scoring + 3 s windows may normalize away absolute velocity (bradykinesia) and stride-to-stride variability — suppressing real pathophysiology along with the confound.
- **Single-site + adaptive CE = latent recording leakage;** cross-site generalization is unverified.
- **Severity unknown (no UPDRS-III / Hoehn-Yahr):** the weak signal could come from a few advanced cases and vanish for early/mild PD — where screening value lies.

Recommendations:
- Treat age as a primary confound on par with duration: age-matched subset + age-residualized analysis, plus an adversarial probe (train the same pipeline to predict age/sex; if it works, quantify how much PD AUC that explains).
- Log/standardize medication state; report a defined-state subgroup.
- Acquire UPDRS-III / Hoehn-Yahr; report severity-stratified performance and the early-PD (H&Y ≤ 2) subgroup.
- Finish the CE-vs-raw |STFT| ablation + Grad-CAM band-attention check before any signal claim; add UMAP to test label- vs subject/age-clustering.
- Preserve an absolute-speed / stride-length feature so reduced velocity isn't z-scored out; test longer/multi-window aggregation; isolate the TUG turn for a PD-specific analysis.
- Scope all claims to in-clinic, single-site, single-device TUG; state cross-site generalization as explicitly untested.
